Findings from this run, all real and all fixed:

[1] GCC REFUSES to compile std::atomic_thread_fence under -fsanitize=thread:

      error: 'atomic_thread_fence' is not supported with '-fsanitize=thread'

    Not a warning - a hard error, so the gcc-tsan configuration could not build
    at all. Combined with ThreadSanitizer's imprecise modelling of standalone
    fences under Clang, this settled the question: the deque now expresses its
    ordering with sequentially consistent operations rather than relaxed
    operations around fences. Equivalent under the standard, buildable by both
    compilers, and verifiable by both sanitizers.

[2] A genuine data race in JobSystem::finish(), found by ThreadSanitizer and
    reproducible on every run. It read job->parent AFTER decrementing the
    completion counter to zero - but zero is exactly the signal that lets the
    owning thread recycle that slot and overwrite the field. Fixed by reading
    the parent before the decrement. Not findable by ordinary testing: it
    produces a wrong pointer only under a precise interleaving.

[3] A deque test failed under AddressSanitizer - not on its correctness
    assertion, which passed 2000 of 2000 rounds, but on its own guard that both
    the owner and a thief must actually win at least once. Under instrumentation
    thread startup dominated the few-instruction window being tested, so the
    race never occurred and the test was silently proving nothing. Fixed by
    having both threads rendezvous immediately before the contended operation.
    The guard was correct to fail; the test was not creating contention.
